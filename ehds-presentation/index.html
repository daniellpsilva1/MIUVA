The European Health Data Space

A common EU framework for using health data in care and reusing it for research and public benefit.

EIT Health Think Tank (2024), chapters 4–5 · Regulation (EU) 2025/327

Open with a concrete change: a record that can move across borders changes what care and research are possible. The EHDS is a framework, not a database. It joins primary and secondary use under common rules. It sets EHR-system interoperability requirements and distinguishes the framework, the infrastructures (MyHealth@EU for care, HealthData@EU for reuse), and the data holders (hospitals, registries, laboratories). Do not list articles here; make the audience curious about how it works. Spend about 1 minute. Transition: the central distinction is purpose.

Reg. (EU) 2025/327, Arts. 5–9, 53

The central distinction: the same record can serve care and research, and purpose determines the rules. A doctor abroad treating Sofia is primary use — the fact that the data crossed a border does not turn care into research. The same record later used to study a cohort is secondary use. The record does not change; the purpose does. Reveal the classification with a click or the space bar. Keep the corrective explanation short: purpose, not geography, defines the use. About 1 minute 30 seconds. Transition: if secondary use is different, how does a researcher actually get access?

How would a researcher get access?

Question & catalogue

The researcher frames a question and searches discoverable dataset inventories. No data are requested yet — the catalogue reveals what exists, where it is held, and under what conditions.

Application to the HDAB

The user submits an application to the Health Data Access Body: the purpose, the data requested, and the proposed analysis. The HDAB is the gatekeeper — not the data holder, not the researcher.

Assessment & conditional permit

The HDAB checks: is the purpose permitted? Is the data necessary, or could anonymised data suffice? Are opt-outs respected? If yes, a conditional permit is issued and the data holder prepares the minimised dataset.

Analysis in a secure environment

Inside the secure boundary Personal data Analysis Researcher
Outside Raw data — blocked

Reviewed non-personal output

Only non-personal outputs — anonymised statistics, reviewed models — leave the secure environment. The data themselves stay. Download of personal data is not the normal mode of EHDS secondary use.

The data do not leave. Only reviewed, non-personal results do.

Reg. (EU) 2025/327, Arts. 66–73 · Teaching illustration, not a permit service

Secondary use is governed access, not open access. Step through the pathway: question and catalogue, application, HDAB assessment, permit, secure environment, reviewed non-personal output. Emphasise the secure-environment boundary: the data themselves do not leave; only reviewed non-personal outputs do. This is the most misunderstood point. The optional research/marketing comparison shows "potentially eligible; assessment required", not automatic approval. Label the whole scene as a teaching illustration. About 2 minutes. Reg. (EU) 2025/327, Arts. 66–73, 71

Correct the common conflation of pseudonymisation with anonymisation. Pseudonymisation replaces identifiers with codes but allows re-linking through a separately held key — it is still personal data. Anonymisation makes individuals no longer identifiable. Removing a name alone does neither: other fields like date of birth and postcode can re-identify. Do not simulate anonymisation as a cosmetic toggle; the side-by-side comparison makes the distinction concrete. About 1 minute 30 seconds. EIT Health (2024), ch. 4 · Fictional teaching example

The key teaching example. Three records: A (7.0 mmol/L, fasting), B (126 mg/dL, fasting unknown), C (qualitative note). Step 1: the units are approximately equivalent — conversion is a multiplication by about 18. Step 2: harmonising units does not recover B's missing fasting status, and C has no number to convert. Step 3: harmonisation fixes syntax, not context, missingness, or bias. Let the example breathe; the missing-context point is the key. No medical diagnosis is implied. About 2 minutes. Interoperability makes data exchangeable. It does not make them correct, complete, or representative. Data quality is fitness for a specific question.

EIT Health (2024), ch. 4 · Reg. (EU) 2025/327, Art. 78

Distinguish interoperability from quality, and quality from fitness for a specific question. The passport contrasts exchange and meaning — interoperability — with validity, completeness, provenance, and coverage — quality. Counting recorded tests is not the same as studying treatment effectiveness. Harmonisation does not remove selection bias. Do not over-explain FAIR; the passport makes the point. EIT Health (2024), chs. 4–5

Making records reusable is work, and most of it falls on healthcare organisations. Structured entry, recording once, metadata, funded support, validated text extraction. Clinical care remains the priority. The Ireland/Spain contrast is dated to 2023 consultations — not a permanent ranking. The point is unequal starting points under common deadlines. Name the workload; do not dismiss it as logistics. About 1 minute 30 seconds. Transition: even if the data are ready and the research is done, what has to happen before it changes care?

A permitted analysis is not a validated intervention.

EIT Health (2024), ch. 5 · Closing the loop

The loop: research, evaluation, adoption, monitoring, and back. Each step can fail. An association is not a causal claim; more data does not by itself produce causal evidence. Evaluation turns a finding into a candidate intervention. Adoption requires training and reimbursement. Monitoring closes the loop back to data. Emphasise the evaluation/adoption gap — this is where the loop most often breaks. A permitted analysis is not a validated intervention. About 1 minute 45 seconds. Transition: step back — what should we conclude?

Common rules. Unequal readiness.

The EHDS joins data collection and benefit under common rules — a path from care data to research data more coherent than the patchwork it replaces.

EIT Health (2024), chs. 4–5 — stakeholder recommendations

Implementation depends on resources, standards, and capability. Common deadlines apply to countries at very different stages. Adequate funding is not guaranteed.

EIT Health (2024), chs. 4–5 — observed readiness, not measured outcomes

EIT Health (2024), chs. 4–5 · Regulation (EU) 2025/327

A balanced assessment, not a card grid. Two editorial statements separated by a rule. First: the framework joins data collection and benefit under common rules — a coherent path. Second: implementation depends on resources, standards, and capability; common deadlines apply to countries at very different stages; adequate funding is not guaranteed. Distinguish stakeholder recommendations from measured outcomes. Do not claim the regulation proves implementation success, nor that funding is categorically absent. About 1 minute 15 seconds.
